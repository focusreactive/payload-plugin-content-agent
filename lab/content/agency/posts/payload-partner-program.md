## Practical foundations for content teams

A content model should make routine publishing predictable without preventing teams from building distinctive pages. That means deciding which fields are required, where content can be reused and how editors should handle assets.

Our open-source {{short}} Starter supports Next.js with a headless CMS. The current version is 3.2, and developers can install it with `npx create-headless-starter`. Its [guide to handling images]({{site}}/docs/guides/image-handling/) explains an important part of the publishing workflow: getting images ready for use across a site.

## Planning a move from WordPress

Moving to a headless CMS is not just a matter of transferring page text. A migration plan needs to account for the content people maintain, the URLs visitors already use and the integrations that keep the site working.

Our [migration checklist]({{site}}/blog/wordpress-to-headless-migration-checklist/) sets out the work we consider before a move. Key questions include:

- Which content should be migrated, rewritten or retired?
- How will existing URLs and redirects be handled?
- Who needs to review content and test publishing before launch?

Answering these early helps teams define the scope before choosing how to move each piece of content.

## Choosing where to host

A headless project also needs a deployment setup that suits its application and operating requirements. Teams should consider build behaviour, image delivery, environment variables and the day-to-day work of maintaining the site.

For a broader look at the options, our [hosting platform roundup]({{site}}/blog/best-nextjs-hosting-platforms-2026/) examines choices for Next.js projects in 2026. Hosting is part of the planning conversation from the start, rather than a decision left until launch.
