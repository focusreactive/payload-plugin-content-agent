## The client

Verdan Health is a healthcare company based in Germany. Its website serves audiences across six languages, with content that must remain clear and consistent as local teams adapt it for their markets.

In 2024, Verdan Health worked with {{brand}} on a migration and localization project. The aim was to bring existing pages into a shared content platform without treating every language as a separate website. [The agency’s team]({{site}}/about/) brought experience from its offices in Amsterdam and Berlin.

## The challenge

The existing content had grown across markets, making it difficult for editors to see how pages related to one another. Moving the site meant preserving useful material while giving teams a more reliable way to maintain it.

The project had several connected problems:

- Pages needed to be accounted for before migration, including their language variants and relationships to other content.
- Local teams needed room to adapt copy without rebuilding page structures for every market.
- Shared content needed clear ownership so that a change in one language did not become an unnoticed inconsistency elsewhere.
- Editors needed a publishing process that made review visible before healthcare information went live.

A simple page-by-page transfer would have moved the content without addressing those editorial problems. The work therefore had to cover both the migration and the way Verdan Health would manage localized content afterward.

## The approach

{{brand}} organized the work around the content itself, rather than starting with a visual redesign.

1. **Audit and map.** The teams reviewed the existing pages, identified language variants and mapped content into a shared structure. This established what needed to move and where editors would maintain it.
2. **Model and migrate.** They defined reusable page components and fields, then transferred content into the new platform. Migration checks compared the source material with the new entries so that missing or misplaced content could be corrected.
3. **Localize and review.** Local teams reviewed language variants in context, checking copy, links and page structure before publication.
4. **Launch and hand over.** The teams tested the publishing workflow and prepared editors to maintain pages after launch.

This sequence gave migration and localization a common framework. Instead of asking translators to work from disconnected page copies, Verdan Health could organize language work around content that had already been mapped and reviewed.

## The solution

The platform uses [Storyblok](https://www.storyblok.com) to manage content and [Nuxt](https://nuxt.com) to deliver the website. Storyblok provides the editorial environment for structured content, while the Nuxt application renders that content for visitors.

Reusable components give editors a consistent way to assemble pages. Fields for headings, body copy and links keep content distinct from presentation, while page structures provide a common starting point for localized variants. That makes it easier to review a page as a whole without forcing every market to publish identical wording.

The migration work included checking content placement and relationships between pages. Editors could then review each language version against the relevant source material and resolve issues before publishing. The process also gave local teams a clearer place to flag copy that needed adaptation rather than direct translation.

The result was a platform designed around ongoing editorial work, not just the launch. A shared model supports consistency, while localization remains a deliberate review task for the people responsible for each market.
