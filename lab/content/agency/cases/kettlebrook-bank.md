## The client

Kettlebrook Bank is a UK fintech company with a website serving people looking for information about its services. Its content needed to be easy to find, clear to navigate and manageable for the team responsible for keeping it current.

For its 2024 launch, the bank worked with {{brand}} on an audit and migration to [Contentful](https://www.contentful.com) and [Next.js](https://nextjs.org). The brief covered both the content behind the site and the experience delivered to visitors.

{{brand}} has worked on headless projects since its founding in 2015. Its Amsterdam and [Berlin office]({{site}}/blog/berlin-office-opening/) teams bring together the content and technical disciplines needed for migrations of this kind.

## The challenge

Moving a large website is not simply a matter of copying text into a new system. A page may depend on its URL, links from other pages, metadata and reusable content. Each of those details needs attention if visitors and search engines are to find the right information after launch.

The audit focused the migration on the problems most likely to affect the bank’s publishing workflow and search presence:

- Existing content needed to be accounted for before it could be moved or reviewed.
- Page structures and reusable content needed a consistent model in the new CMS.
- URLs and internal links needed to remain useful after the move.
- Search metadata needed to travel with the content it described.
- The new site needed to render pages efficiently without making editing unnecessarily complicated.

The scale of the content estate made it important to treat editorial review and technical delivery as connected work. An accurate content model would be of limited use if links broke during launch; equally, a fast site would not solve the bank’s problem if editors could not maintain its pages.

## The approach

{{brand}} structured the work around an audit, a migration plan and checks on the resulting site. The approach gave the team a way to trace content from its existing location to its place in the new publishing system.

1. **Audit the content and structure.** Review the page inventory alongside URLs, metadata and relationships between pages. Identify the information needed to reproduce each page in the new system.
2. **Define the content model.** Organise pages and reusable elements in Contentful so that editors can maintain content without treating every page as an isolated build.
3. **Migrate and validate.** Move the content into the new model, then check the rendered pages, links and metadata against the migration plan.
4. **Prepare for launch.** Review the handling of existing URLs and the performance of the Next.js site before making the new experience available.

This sequence kept the migration tied to the bank’s publishing needs rather than letting the CMS structure dictate the website. The same principle applies in other headless work, including [commerce projects]({{site}}/headless-ecommerce/), where content and presentation also need to work together.

## The solution

Contentful provides the place for the bank’s team to manage structured content. Next.js uses that content to render the website. Separating the content from the presentation gives editors a clearer view of what they maintain while allowing the site’s interface to be developed around visitors’ needs.

The content model was central to the migration. Page content, metadata and reusable material needed identifiable places in the CMS so they could be checked and maintained after launch. The migration work also considered the connections between pages: a page is easier to preserve when its destination, links and purpose are understood together.

Search visibility depended on more than moving body copy. The project treated URLs and metadata as part of the content record, with validation used to check that the new site presented the intended information. That made the audit relevant throughout delivery, not just at the start.

Performance was another part of the implementation. Next.js gave the team a framework for building the public-facing experience, while page checks helped assess how the migrated content was delivered.

## What comes next

The migration gives the bank a structured foundation for maintaining its website after launch. Its team can continue to review content, update information and monitor the pages that visitors rely on.

For future changes, the audit and content model provide a useful reference: editors can assess where new material belongs, how it relates to existing pages and what metadata it needs. Continued attention to URLs, internal links and performance will help the bank maintain the experience established by the migration.
