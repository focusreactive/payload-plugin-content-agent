## The client

Nordlys Outdoor is a Norwegian ecommerce company serving customers across four markets. In 2025, it worked with {{brand}} to migrate and develop a headless storefront: a customer-facing site separated from the systems used to manage products and editorial content.

The project brought together [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com) for commerce, [Sanity](https://www.sanity.io) for content management and [Next.js](https://nextjs.org) for the storefront. The goal was to give Nordlys Outdoor a way to manage its shopping experience across markets without treating every change as a separate site project.

## The challenge

The migration needed to protect the everyday work of selling products while giving the team more control over how those products were presented. Nordlys Outdoor also needed a storefront that could serve multiple markets without forcing editors to rebuild the same material for each one.

The main problems were:

- Product information and editorial storytelling needed to work together on the same shopping journey.
- Market-specific content had to be manageable alongside shared content.
- The existing storefront needed to be replaced without losing sight of product discovery and purchase paths.
- Page performance needed attention across the customer-facing experience.
- The migration had to move content and commerce into a new architecture that the team could maintain after launch.

These were not just development questions. The project also needed clear decisions about where information belonged, who would maintain it and how it would reach the storefront.

## The approach

{{brand}} delivered the migration and development work in phases, moving from the shape of the content to the implementation of the storefront.

1. **Map the existing experience.** The team reviewed the information needed for browsing and buying, including product details, navigation and editorial content. This established what needed to move and what the new storefront needed to display.
2. **Define content responsibilities.** Shopify remained the commerce system, while Sanity provided a place to structure content for the storefront. The content model accounted for material shared across markets and material that needed a market-specific version.
3. **Build the storefront.** The Next.js frontend brought commerce data and editorial content together in the pages customers use to discover and evaluate products.
4. **Migrate and check content.** Content was prepared for the new model and reviewed in the context of the pages where it would appear. Navigation, product presentation and market-specific content were part of that review.
5. **Launch the experience.** The team checked the connected storefront and its purchase paths before the 2025 launch, then handed over an architecture Nordlys Outdoor could continue to manage.

Choosing a content system is one part of this kind of work; designing the editing workflow is another. For teams weighing their options, {{brand}} has also examined [how Storyblok and Contentful compare for marketing teams]({{site}}/blog/storyblok-vs-contentful/).

## The solution

The new storefront separates the presentation layer from the systems that hold commerce and editorial information. Shopify supports the commerce side, Sanity organizes content, and Next.js renders the customer-facing experience.

That separation gives the team room to shape product pages around more than a standard product record. Editorial content can sit alongside commerce information, helping customers understand products in the context of the outdoor activities they are shopping for. The storefront can present those elements together while the underlying information remains in its respective system.

The content model also gives Nordlys Outdoor a way to distinguish shared material from market-specific material. Instead of assuming every market needs an entirely separate experience, editors can work with content according to where it belongs. That makes the structure useful for maintaining consistency as well as making local changes.

The result is an editing setup intended to support ongoing work, not just the launch. Related considerations about editorial workflows appear in {{brand}}’s [survey of what content teams want]({{site}}/blog/editor-experience-survey-2026/).

## What comes next

With the storefront live, Nordlys Outdoor can continue to develop its content and shopping experience within the new structure. The separation between commerce, content and presentation gives the team a basis for updating editorial material and market-specific pages without rebuilding the entire storefront.

For future changes, the important discipline is to keep the content model and customer experience aligned: product information should remain dependable, editorial additions should have a clear purpose, and market-specific differences should be easy for the team to identify and maintain.
