=== richTextSection
heading: A practical route to headless in Germany

A headless CMS separates content management from the frontend that displays it. That gives your team a way to reuse structured content across websites and other digital channels, while developers can choose the frontend technology that fits the project.

From our Berlin office, {{brand}} works with companies in Germany on the decisions that make that separation useful. We start with the content and the people who publish it, then define the CMS, integrations and frontend around their needs. Our team can also draw on experience from our Amsterdam office.

A typical engagement addresses:

- Content types, relationships and editorial responsibilities
- CMS selection and integration with existing systems
- Migration mapping, review and launch planning
- Frontend development, including Next.js where appropriate
- Training and a maintainable handover for your team

Migration scope depends on the number and condition of your existing content types, as well as the integrations and review work involved. For context when planning a budget, explore [our 2025 CMS migration cost data]({{site}}/blog/cms-migration-cost-report-2025/).

We also maintain {{short}} Starter, an open source starter kit for Next.js and a headless CMS. Its current version is 3.2, and you can install it with `npx create-headless-starter`. It provides a starting point for development rather than a substitute for content modeling and project planning.
